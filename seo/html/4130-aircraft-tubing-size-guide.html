Condition N: The Aircraft Mandate

Before discussing size, aircraft builders must understand condition. For certificated aircraft, sizing is irrelevant if the condition is wrong. The tubing must meet AMS-T-6736, which explicitly requires Condition N (Normalized). As-drawn mechanical tubing (ASTM A519) does not meet this requirement, regardless of whether the physical dimensions are correct.

Fuselage Truss Sizing Logic

In a classic Warren truss welded steel fuselage, sizes change throughout the airframe based on the load path:
- Engine Mount & Forward Cabin: Heaviest tubing (e.g., 0.750" to 1.000" OD, 0.049" to 0.065" wall) to support the engine weight, torque, and landing gear loads.
- Tailcone / Aft Fuselage: Progressively lighter tubing (e.g., 0.500" to 0.625" OD, 0.035" wall) as loads decrease toward the tail, critical for maintaining the aircraft's center of gravity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I substitute a slightly thicker wall if the exact aircraft size is unavailable?

In experimental aviation, builders sometimes substitute the next wall thickness up (e.g., 0.049 instead of 0.035) if a size is unavailable. While structurally safe, this adds weight. In certificated aircraft repair, substitutions must be approved by the design authority or IA in accordance with AC 43.13-1B.
