AMS-T-6736 — The Aircraft Structural Tubing Standard

For FAA-certificated aircraft, the primary material specification for 4130 structural tubing is AMS-T-6736 (formerly MIL-T-6736). This standard mandates:
- Grade: 4130 chromium-molybdenum steel
- Condition: Normalized (Condition N)
- Form: Seamless
- Dimensional tolerances: Defined within the standard
- Testing: Chemical analysis, mechanical properties

AMS-T-6736 imposes requirements beyond standard ASTM A519 mechanical tubing, including the normalized condition requirement and specific mechanical property ranges. Buyers sourcing 4130 for FAA-certificated repair, replacement, or new construction must verify that the tubing meets AMS-T-6736 and that the MTC confirms compliance.

Experimental Aircraft

For FAA Experimental Amateur-Built (EAB) aircraft, the material requirements are determined by the builder and the type design (if following an existing design). Many EAB designs specify 4130 CDS to ASTM A519 rather than AMS-T-6736. The builder-PIC is responsible for determining the appropriate specification for their design category and must document the material used.

Frequently Asked Questions

Must I use AMS-T-6736 for all aircraft 4130 tubing?

For FAA-certificated aircraft structural applications, yes — the applicable maintenance/repair standards (e.g., AC 43.13-1B) direct the use of AMS-T-6736 or its equivalent. For experimental aircraft, the builder determines the specification. Verify requirements with the applicable authority or designer.

Is normalized condition always required for aircraft 4130?

AMS-T-6736 specifically requires the normalized condition. Annealed or as-drawn 4130 does not meet AMS-T-6736 requirements. The MTC must explicitly state 'Condition N' or 'Normalized' to confirm compliance.
