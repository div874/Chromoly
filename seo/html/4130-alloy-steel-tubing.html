What 'Alloy Steel' Means for 4130

Alloy steels are defined by the deliberate addition of alloying elements beyond what is found in plain carbon steel. For 4130, the defining additions are chromium (approximately 0.80–1.10%) and molybdenum (approximately 0.15–0.25%), with manganese and silicon also present. These additions modify the steel's response to heat treatment, improve toughness, and increase hardenability — allowing higher strength to be achieved in section sizes where plain carbon steel would not respond adequately to quenching.

Procurement and Material Control Classification

In quality management systems (ISO 9001, AS9100), material control procedures classify incoming materials. 4130 tubing is classified as: alloy steel (not carbon steel), seamless (not welded), mechanical or structural tubing (not pipe). Correctly classifying the material in the procurement system avoids misidentification during receiving inspection or traceability audits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is alloy steel tubing different from carbon steel tubing?

Carbon steel tubing (e.g., ASTM A513 / 1020) contains no deliberate additions of chromium, molybdenum, or other significant alloying elements beyond what is present incidentally. Alloy steel tubing (e.g., 4130) has intentional chromium and molybdenum additions that increase hardenability and strength beyond what plain carbon steel can achieve.
