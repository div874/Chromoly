What Makes 4130 Distinct

Compared with standard carbon steel, the alloying additions in 4130 — chromium (approximately 0.80–1.10%) and molybdenum (approximately 0.15–0.25%) — increase hardenability, improve toughness, and allow the steel to achieve higher yield strength in heat-treated conditions. The carbon content is low enough to retain reasonable weldability, making it practical for fabricated structures.

Manufacturing Form

The standard supply form for structural and aerospace-grade 4130 is Cold Drawn Seamless (CDS). The seamless manufacturing process — piercing a solid billet rather than welding a formed strip — results in a tube with no longitudinal weld seam. This is important for applications where isotropic strength across the full cross-section is required, or where the applicable standard mandates seamless manufacture.

Applicable Standards

4130 seamless tubing is commonly supplied to ASTM A519 (Seamless Carbon and Alloy Steel Mechanical Tubing). For aerospace applications, AMS-T-6736 (formerly MIL-T-6736) governs. The EN equivalent for seamless mechanical tubes is EN 10297. Buyers should specify the applicable standard at the enquiry stage, as the dimensional and testing requirements vary between specifications.

Ordering Requirements

A complete purchase specification for 4130 chromoly tubing should include: Outside Diameter (OD) in decimal inches or millimetres, wall thickness, total footage or weight, manufacturing form (Cold Drawn Seamless), applicable standard or condition (e.g., Normalized), and documentation requirement (EN 10204 3.1 MTC). Incomplete specifications result in re-quoting delays or incorrect material being supplied.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does '4130' mean in the grade designation?

The first two digits (41) identify the alloy family — chromium-molybdenum. The last two digits (30) indicate the nominal carbon content in hundredths of a percent, so 0.30% nominal carbon.

Is 4130 the same as chromoly?

'Chromoly' is an informal term for chromium-molybdenum alloy steel. 4130 is the most widely used chromoly tubing grade. Not all chromoly steel is 4130 — 4140 and 4340 are also chromoly grades — but in the context of tubing, '4130 chromoly' refers specifically to this grade.

Can 4130 tubing be welded?

Yes. 4130 has adequate weldability for TIG (GTAW) welding using appropriate filler metal and technique. Thicker walls or high-constraint joints may require pre-heating and post-weld heat treatment. The applicable welding procedure specification should be consulted for the specific application.
