Cold Rolled vs. Cold Drawn

Cold rolling and cold drawing are distinct metalworking operations:

- Cold rolling reduces thickness by passing material between rolls. This is applied to flat products (sheet, strip) and, before forming, to strip that will be shaped into welded tube.
- Cold drawing reduces tube diameter and wall thickness by pulling the tube through a die over a mandrel at ambient temperature. This is the finishing operation for seamless tubes.

For seamless 4130 alloy tubing, the correct term is Cold Drawn Seamless (CDS). A buyer asking for 'cold rolled 4130 tubing' may in fact be seeking CDS seamless tube — or may be looking for DOM (Drawn Over Mandrel) mild steel, which starts as cold-rolled strip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 'cold rolled 4130 tubing' a real product?

Not as a standard commercial tubing product. The correct form of cold-worked 4130 alloy tubing is Cold Drawn Seamless (CDS). If your drawing or specification says 'cold rolled 4130 tube', verify whether 'cold drawn seamless' was intended.
