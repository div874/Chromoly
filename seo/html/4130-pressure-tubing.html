Pressure Calculation Basics

The working pressure of a tube is primarily a function of wall thickness, OD, material yield or tensile strength, the applicable design code, and the safety factor required. The fundamental relationship — the Barlow formula or the code-specific equivalent — provides allowable working pressure as a function of these parameters. For a given OD and material, increasing wall thickness increases the pressure rating; using a higher-strength material (such as 4130 vs. mild steel) also allows higher pressure for the same wall.

Standards for Pressure Service

ASTM A519 mechanical tubing is not a pressure vessel standard. For pressure-critical applications, the engineer must determine the applicable code — ASME B31.3 (Process Piping), ASME Section VIII Division 1 (Pressure Vessels), or an application-specific standard — and ensure the tube material, condition, and testing meet that code's requirements. ASTM A519 may be acceptable as the base material; however, additional testing or inspection requirements may apply under the pressure code.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use 4130 ASTM A519 tube for a boiler pressure vessel?

ASTM A519 is a mechanical tubing standard. Boiler and pressure vessel applications are governed by ASME codes, which have specific approved material lists. ASTM A519 may not be the appropriate specification for ASME boiler work — consult an ASME-qualified engineer.
