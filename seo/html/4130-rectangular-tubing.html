Standard Commercial Reality

Rectangular hollow sections (RHS) in structural steel are commonly produced in lower-carbon welded steel (e.g., ASTM A500, EN 10219). These are not 4130 alloy. If a design specifies 4130 alloy in a rectangular cross-section, it typically reflects a design intent that should be reviewed for feasibility:

1. Can the same load case be achieved with a round 4130 tube of equivalent section modulus?
2. Can a heavier-wall round tube achieve the required stiffness?
3. Is the 4130 alloy actually required, or can a structural steel RHS meet the load requirement with adequate safety factor?

When to Seek a Design Review

Non-standard cross-section specifications in safety-critical structures should always be reviewed by a qualified engineer before procurement. Changing a round tube design to a rectangular section (or vice versa) changes the load path, joint geometry, and section efficiency in ways that require structural recalculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there any rectangular hollow sections made from chromoly-equivalent steel?

Some specialised fabricators can produce rectangular sections from 4130 or similar alloy by welding formed plate. This is not a standard commercial product and involves significant cost and lead time premium. Standard round CDS 4130 should be used wherever possible.
