Custom and Kit Car Context

Custom car builders and kit car fabricators choose 4130 for its higher yield strength — which allows lighter tube for the same structural requirement compared to mild steel. The trade-off is weld skill requirement: 4130 benefits from TIG welding by a skilled operator, particularly at highly-stressed joint areas. MIG welding is possible but requires appropriate wire and technique.

For road-legal vehicles, the chassis must meet the applicable road vehicle regulations in the jurisdiction (e.g., SVA/IVA in the UK, state vehicle inspection in the US). These regulations do not typically specify 4130, but the builder's structural analysis must demonstrate that the chassis is adequate.

Tube Sizing for Custom Chassis

Custom chassis tube sizing is a structural engineering task. General reference: main frame rails in a lightweight custom car often use 1.5"–2.0" OD square or round section; body structure uses smaller diameter round tube. However, these are starting points — a proper chassis requires load analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I MIG weld 4130 for a custom car chassis?

MIG (GMAW) welding is possible for 4130 using appropriate wire and technique. However, TIG (GTAW) is preferred for structural joints because it provides better control of heat input and produces higher-quality welds in thin-wall 4130 tubing. For safety-critical chassis joints, TIG welding by a skilled operator is recommended.
