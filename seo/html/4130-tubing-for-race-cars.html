Primary Structural Systems

A typical circuit race car uses 4130 tubing in three overlapping structural systems:

1. The safety cage: Designed to the sanctioning body's roll cage rules. Non-negotiable in geometry, size, and material.

2. The chassis structure: The primary frame on which all suspension pickups, drivetrain mounts, and bodywork attach. Sized by the chassis designer based on torsional and bending load analysis.

3. Secondary structural members: Floor cross-members, seat mounting tubes, fuel cell cage, battery mount, and similar elements that support components and distribute loads.

Ordering Complete Tube Kits

Professional race teams and competent amateur builders often source tube in a single consolidated order rather than purchasing piecemeal. This approach: simplifies MTC documentation (fewer heat lots), reduces per-piece cost through volume, and ensures consistent material throughout the build.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the entire race car chassis need to be 4130, or just the cage?

Safety regulations specify 4130 for the roll cage (in classes where 4130 is required). The wider chassis structure may be 4130 or mild steel depending on the designer's choice and the applicable class regulations. Many builders use 4130 throughout for consistency, weight saving, and documentation simplicity.
