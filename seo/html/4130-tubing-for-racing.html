Racing Applications by Structure

Different racing vehicle structures use 4130 in different configurations:

- Roll cage and safety structures: Governed by sanctioning body rules; specific minimum sizes required.
- Spaceframe chassis: Engineer-analysed tube sizes for primary frame members, with lighter secondary bracing.
- Suspension pickup tubes: Short tube sections that form the outer housing of bearing or rose joint assemblies; often machined from solid bar rather than tube.
- Push/pull rods (open-wheel): Axially loaded members where the thin-wall tube carries compression/tension; size determined by column buckling analysis.
- Fuel cell / tank frames: Structural cage protecting the fuel cell; sized for crash protection.

Mixed Sizes — Ordering Strategy

A complete racing build typically requires multiple tube sizes. Ordering strategy:
1. Compile a complete cut list from your design or plans
2. Sum footage per OD/wall combination
3. Add 10–15% waste allowance for cuts, miters, and mistakes
4. Order all sizes together to simplify MTC documentation and logistics

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I mix 4130 tubing from different heats in one cage?

Mechanically, yes — if both heats are 4130 to the same specification. However, for traceability purposes, keep the MTC for each heat in your build documentation. Using a single heat lot simplifies documentation.
