Material Selection Rationale

Roll cage material selection involves a straightforward engineering trade-off: mild steel DOM (ASTM A513) is cheaper and more forgiving of amateur welding, but requires heavier walls to meet the same structural requirement as 4130. Most sanctioning bodies recognise this by permitting lighter-gauge 4130 where heavier DOM would otherwise be required.

For competitive motorsport where every kilogram of structural weight is scrutinised, 4130 CDS is the professional standard.

What Rulebooks Typically Specify

Sanctioning body rules typically prescribe: minimum OD for each cage element (main hoop, door bars, diagonal), minimum wall thickness (which differs between 4130 and DOM in most rulebooks), and the requirement for seamless construction. The specific values vary by sanctioning body, year, and vehicle class. Always reference the current official rulebook — not historical data or internet forums.

Ordering for Roll Cage Builds

For a typical motorsport roll cage, the builder needs multiple OD and wall combinations — main hoop, door bars, and diagonal braces are often different sizes. Order all required sizes together, specifying CDS 4130 and requesting MTC for each size. Keep the MTC with the vehicle for tech inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does 4130 roll cage tubing need to be normalised?

Most motorsport rulebooks do not specify the heat treatment condition for roll cage tubing — they specify the material grade (4130) and minimum dimensions. As-drawn CDS 4130 is widely used. For aircraft applications, AMS-T-6736 requires normalised condition. Verify your specific rulebook.

Where should I store the MTC after building the cage?

Keep a copy of the MTC with the vehicle logbook. Some tech inspectors request it at scrutineering. At minimum, retain it in your build documentation for any future inspection, insurance, or compliance query.
