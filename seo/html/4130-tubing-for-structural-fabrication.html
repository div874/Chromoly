When to Choose 4130 Over Mild Steel

The decision framework for structural tube material:

- If the structure can accommodate a larger OD or heavier wall in mild steel without weight penalty: use mild steel (lower cost, easier welding).
- If weight, compactness, or strength requirements make mild steel inadequate at practical cross-sections: specify 4130 CDS.
- If the structure must be welded by semi-skilled operators: mild steel is more forgiving of welding imperfections.
- If the structure has fatigue loading and requires high yield strength: 4130 provides better fatigue performance at equivalent section size.

Fabrication Notes for 4130

Structural fabrication in 4130 requires attention to:
- Weld process: TIG (GTAW) for quality joints; MIG with appropriate wire as a secondary option.
- Pre-heat: Not required for thin-wall 4130 in ambient conditions; recommended for wall thicknesses above approximately 0.120" or in cold environments.
- Filler metal: ER70S-2 or ER80S-D2 — match to joint requirements.
- Post-weld treatment: Not required for most structural applications; post-weld normalising recommended for highly stressed or impact-loaded structures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can 4130 tube be MIG welded for structural fabrication?

MIG welding of 4130 is possible using appropriate wire (ER80S-D2 or similar) and technique. For structural joints, TIG is preferred for quality control. MIG may be acceptable for secondary members and non-critical joints with appropriate procedure qualification.
