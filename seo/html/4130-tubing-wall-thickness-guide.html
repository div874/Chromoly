Factors Influencing Wall Selection

1. Regulatory Minimums: In motorsport and aerospace, sanctioning bodies define absolute minimum wall thicknesses for specific applications (e.g., 0.083" or 0.120" for roll cage main hoops based on vehicle weight).
2. Welding Considerations: Walls below 0.049" require high TIG welding proficiency to avoid burn-through. Walls above 0.120" generally require pre-heating to prevent heat-affected zone cracking.
3. Local Buckling: A very thin wall on a very large OD (a high OD/t ratio) is structurally inefficient as the tube wall may buckle or crumple under bending load before the material reaches its yield strength.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the minimum wall thickness stated in a rulebook as the nominal order dimension?

Use caution. ASTM A519 allows for wall thickness manufacturing tolerances (typically +10%/-0% or similar depending on size). If a rulebook mandates an absolute minimum wall (e.g., 0.118" minimum measured by sonic tester), you should order a nominal wall that guarantees that minimum (e.g., 0.120" nominal) and verify the mill tolerance.
