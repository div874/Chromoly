What an MTC Is — and Is Not

An MTC is:
- A document produced by the steel mill
- A record of actual test results from specific testing performed on the heat
- Certified by the manufacturer's authorised representative (Type 3.1) or also countersigned by an independent party (Type 3.2)
- The traceability link between physical material and documented compliance

An MTC is NOT:
- A general marketing declaration that the material is 'high quality'
- A guarantee of all mechanical properties unless those tests are included
- A substitute for an engineering calculation or design validation
- Produced retroactively for material that was not tested at the time of manufacture

How to Verify the MTC

Receiving inspection for 4130 tubing with MTC should include:

1. Confirm the MTC accompanies the delivery — not a photocopy of a generic document.
2. Match the heat number on the MTC to the heat number marked on the tube bundle or end tag.
3. Compare the ladle analysis element by element against the applicable specification limits (from ASTM A519 for the Grade 4130 composition table).
4. Confirm the product form (seamless CDS) matches the order.
5. Confirm the condition (if specified) is recorded on the MTC.
6. File the MTC by heat number for QMS traceability.

MTC vs. EN 10204 3.1 vs. Third-Party Inspection

The terminology is sometimes confused:

- MTC: the document itself (the Mill Test Certificate)
- EN 10204 3.1: the certification type — specifies that the MTC is certified by the manufacturer's authorised representative
- Third-party inspection (EN 10204 3.2): the MTC is also countersigned by an independent inspection body

For most industrial applications, an EN 10204 3.1 MTC is the standard requirement. Some aerospace, defence, and pressure vessel programmes require 3.2. Neither level should be assumed — state the requirement explicitly at the purchase order stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if the MTC ladle analysis does not include all elements?

Some MTCs present a partial analysis. For 4130, the key elements to verify are carbon, chromium, and molybdenum — these define the 4130 alloy identity. If these elements are present and within specification limits, the MTC is generally sufficient for most applications. For critical applications, request a full ladle analysis on the purchase order.

Can an MTC be forged or falsified?

Fraudulent MTCs exist as a documented problem in the steel supply chain. Mitigation strategies include: sourcing from reputable suppliers with known supply chain integrity, verifying the heat number against the tube marking, requesting actual test results rather than templated documents, and — for critical applications — using third-party inspection (EN 10204 3.2).

How long should I retain the MTC?

Retention requirements depend on the application. Aerospace and pressure vessel applications typically require MTC retention for the design life of the structure. For motorsport applications, retain the MTC for the life of the safety structure. At minimum, retain for the duration of any warranty or contractual obligation period.
