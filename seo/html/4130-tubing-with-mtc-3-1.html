What EN 10204 3.1 MTC Documents

An EN 10204 3.1 MTC for 4130 tubing typically records:

- Material grade (AISI 4130 or EN 25CrMo4 / 1.7218)
- Applicable specification (ASTM A519, AMS-T-6736, or EN 10297)
- Product form (seamless, CDS, or HFS)
- Dimensions (OD, wall, length)
- Heat/cast number (the manufacturing batch identifier)
- Ladle analysis (actual chemical composition of the heat)
- Mechanical test results (if required by specification or buyer)
- Condition/heat treatment (if applicable)
- Signatory: the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative

The document is not signed by an independent third party under 3.1 — that is a 3.2 requirement.

How to Use MTC 3.1 in Procurement

On receipt of 4130 tubing, the buyer's receiving inspection should:

1. Confirm the MTC accompanies the delivery
2. Check the grade on the MTC matches the order
3. Verify the heat number appears on both the MTC and the tube bundle (end marking or tag)
4. Review the ladle analysis — compare with the applicable specification composition limits
5. Check the condition / heat treatment if specified
6. File the MTC with the delivery documentation

Frequently Asked Questions

Is MTC 3.1 the same as a Certificate of Conformance?

No. An MTC (Mill Test Certificate) documents actual test results — ladle analysis and, where required, mechanical test data. A Certificate of Conformance is a supplier declaration that the material meets specification. Both may be required by some quality plans. They serve different purposes.

What is the difference between MTC 3.1 and MTC 3.2?

Under EN 10204: 3.1 is certified by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative (internal certification). 3.2 is certified by both the manufacturer and an independent inspection body or buyer's representative (external witness). 3.2 provides an additional layer of independent verification and is required by some aerospace and pressure vessel programmes.
