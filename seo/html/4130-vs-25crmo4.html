Chemical Composition Overlap

Both designations aim for an alloy with approximately 0.25–0.33% Carbon, 0.80–1.20% Chromium, and 0.15–0.25% Molybdenum. The exact allowable ranges in the ASTM and EN specifications overlap significantly but are not perfectly identical.

When a European drawing specifies 25CrMo4, and 4130 is offered as a substitute, the MTC ladle analysis of the 4130 heat must be checked to ensure all elements fall within the allowable limits of the EN 25CrMo4 specification.

Procurement Context

European fabricators routinely use 25CrMo4 for roll cages, chassis, and structural components exactly as North American fabricators use 4130. Many global steel mills produce heats that intentionally fall into the overlapping composition window so the material can be dual-certified to both ASTM A519 (as 4130) and EN 10297 (as 25CrMo4).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use 4130 if my FIA regulations specify 25CrMo4?

Yes, FIA Appendix J typically lists 25CrMo4 and explicitly permits equivalent alloy steels. 4130 is universally accepted as the equivalent. Retain your MTC 3.1 to prove the material is 4130.
