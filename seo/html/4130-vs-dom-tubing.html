The Actual Comparison: 4130 Seamless vs Mild Steel DOM

When fabricators ask about '4130 vs DOM', they are almost always actually asking: 'Should I use 4130 Seamless Tubing or 1020/1026 Mild Steel DOM Tubing?'

- 4130 Seamless (CDS): Higher strength alloy (Cr-Mo), no weld seam, higher cost, requires TIG welding skill. Permitted by all racing sanctioning bodies for roll cages.
- Mild Steel DOM: Standard carbon steel (usually SAE 1020), contains a smoothed longitudinal weld seam, lower cost, easier to MIG weld. Requires heavier walls to achieve the same structural strength as 4130. Prohibited by some high-level racing rules for safety cages.

The DOM Process Explained

DOM tubing starts as a flat sheet of steel that is rolled into a tube and electric-resistance welded (ERW). This welded tube is then drawn through a die and over a mandrel. This cold working process improves dimensional accuracy, increases yield strength, and smooths the weld seam inside and out. However, it is still a welded tube.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy 4130 DOM tubing?

While theoretically possible to manufacture, it is extremely rare. Almost all structural 4130 tubing is Cold Drawn Seamless (CDS). If you need 4130, you should specify CDS. If you need DOM, you are typically buying mild steel.
