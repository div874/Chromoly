AISI 4130 Tubing

AISI 4130 is the American Iron and Steel Institute designation for chromium-molybdenum alloy steel. In tubing form, specifying AISI 4130 defines the chemical composition but must be paired with an appropriate product standard (such as ASTM A519) to establish dimensional and mechanical requirements.

AISI vs. SAE vs. ASTM — What Each Governs

The AISI and SAE systems are grade identification systems. They define the alloy family and nominal composition of the steel, not the product form, dimensions, or testing requirements. ASTM standards (such as A519 for mechanical tubing) govern the product itself — its manufacturing form, dimensional tolerances, mechanical testing, and certification. When specifying AISI 4130 tubing, the AISI designation identifies the grade; a product standard such as ASTM A519 governs how it must be manufactured and tested.

Sourcing to AISI 4130

When a purchase order states 'AISI 4130 tubing' without further standard reference, the supplier must infer or request the applicable product standard, dimensional requirements, and condition. For the buyer, always supplement the AISI grade designation with:
- Product standard (ASTM A519 for mechanical tube)
- Manufacturing form (Cold Drawn Seamless)
- Dimensions (OD and wall)
- Condition (Annealed, Normalized, etc.)
- Documentation (EN 10204 3.1 MTC)

A specification limited to 'AISI 4130' leaves too many parameters undefined.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is AISI 4130 the same as SAE 4130?

Yes. The AISI and SAE numbering systems for steel grades are identical. Both refer to the same chromium-molybdenum alloy composition. The two organisations historically collaborated on the system; the designation is often written 'AISI/SAE 4130'.

Does 'AISI 4130' specify the tubing dimensions?

No. AISI 4130 identifies only the alloy grade and nominal composition. Dimensions, tolerances, and testing requirements are governed by the applicable product standard (such as ASTM A519). Always specify the product standard alongside the grade.
