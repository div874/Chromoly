What 'Certified' Means in Practice

In steel supply, 'certified' material means the material is accompanied by an EN 10204 3.1 (or 3.2) Mill Test Certificate. The certificate:

- Is produced by the steel mill (not the distributor)
- Documents the actual chemical composition of the specific heat
- Documents mechanical test results where required
- Identifies the heat/cast number, linking the certificate to the physical material
- Is signed by the manufacturer's authorised inspection representative (3.1) or also countersigned by an independent party (3.2)

A supplier statement that material is '4130' without a proper MTC is not 'certified' in this sense.

How to Verify Certification

To verify that received 4130 tubing is genuinely certified:

1. The MTC must be provided — not a certificate of conformance alone.
2. The heat number on the MTC must match the heat number stamped or tagged on the tube bundle.
3. The ladle analysis on the MTC must be compared against the applicable specification composition limits.
4. The grade and form stated on the MTC must match the purchase order.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a distributor produce the MTC, or must it come from the mill?

The MTC is produced by the steel mill that manufactured the material — it certifies test results from the production process. A distributor can provide a copy of the original mill MTC, but cannot produce a new MTC from their own authority. Any certificate produced by a distributor claiming to certify mill-level testing should be questioned.
