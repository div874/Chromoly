The Normalizing Process

Normalizing involves heating the 4130 steel above its upper critical temperature (Ac3 — approximately 900°C for 4130) and then allowing it to cool in still air. The air cooling produces a more uniform, fine-grained pearlitic/ferritic microstructure than annealing (which involves slow furnace cooling) and provides a consistent property baseline across the tube cross-section.

For 4130 tube manufactured to AMS-T-6736, the normalizing requirement is explicit: the tube must be supplied in the normalized condition (Condition N). As-drawn or annealed material does not meet AMS-T-6736 requirements.

Properties of Normalized 4130

Normalized 4130 has intermediate yield and tensile strength — higher than annealed, lower than quenched-and-tempered. The normalizing process removes internal stresses from the cold drawing operation and provides a uniform microstructure. The specific properties depend on the heat and the applicable specification — the MTC provides the actual test results.

For safety-critical structural calculations, use the applicable specification minimum values — not MTC values from a specific heat, which may be higher.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is all 4130 CDS tubing automatically normalized?

No. As-drawn 4130 CDS has been cold drawn but not subsequently heat treated. Normalizing is a separate heat treatment operation that must be specified. State 'Normalized' or 'Condition N' explicitly on the purchase order — and verify it is stated on the MTC.

Can normalizing be done after delivery?

Yes — normalizing can be performed by a competent heat treater after delivery of as-drawn tube. For AMS-T-6736 compliance, however, the normalizing must be performed by the manufacturer or an approved heat treater, and the MTC must reflect the normalised condition. Retroactive 'normalizing' and re-issuing the original MTC is not permissible.
