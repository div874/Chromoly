WNr as an Abbreviation

In German technical documents: W = Werkstoff (material), Nr = Nummer (number). 'WNr' is therefore the abbreviated form of 'Werkstoff-Nummer'. It appears in MTC documentation, purchase orders, drawings, and material certifications from German and European sources. Buyers should be aware that 'WNr 1.7218', 'Werkstoff 1.7218', '1.7218', and '25CrMo4' all refer to the same alloy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does documentation sometimes show 'WNr' and sometimes '1.7218' without prefix?

Both are acceptable. Some documents abbreviate 'Werkstoff-Nummer' as WNr; others omit the prefix and list only the number. Both '1.7218' and 'WNr 1.7218' identify the same alloy grade.
